# Missing evidence — what would turn the thesis into proof

The models consistently separated a credible mechanism from an unproved
ecosystem outcome. These are the decisive next proofs, in priority order.

1. **Real multi-engine CI.** Execute the same sealed fixture successfully with
   at least two real engines on every push. This turns vendor-neutrality from a
   design property into observed portability.
2. **Adversarial evaluation quality.** Add sealed holdouts, mutation tests, and
   false-green measurements. This tests whether agents can satisfy the visible
   eval while violating intent.
3. **Independent executors.** Have third-party implementations pass L1/L2
   conformance without relying on the reference executor's assumptions.
4. **Comparative field study.** Compare Task-Spec with ordinary issues plus CI:
   authoring time, retries, acceptance rate, escaped defects, review time, and
   cost per accepted change.
5. **Operational correlation.** Measure whether local acceptance predicts PR
   approval, deployment success, and production behavior. Keep those states
   distinct even if they correlate.
6. **Stronger authority and isolation.** Demonstrate key rotation or
   per-author identity and enforced sandbox/network policy for unattended work.

## Falsification conditions

The thesis weakens materially if:

- real engines require bespoke rewrites of the supposedly portable task;
- authoring and verification cost exceeds the rework it prevents;
- agents repeatedly pass the eval while violating reviewed behavior;
- third-party executors cannot reproduce conformance;
- A2A, MCP, or dominant agent platforms standardize an incompatible task
  contract that users prefer;
- local acceptance does not predict better review or delivery outcomes.

This is the correct closing tension for Day 3: the mechanism is implemented;
the broader ecosystem claim remains a hypothesis with named tests.
